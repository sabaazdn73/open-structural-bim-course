# Exercise 7.5: Clash detection with the MEP model

**Goal.** Find the clashes between your structure and the MEP model, and resolve them with openings.

## What you build

- Run a clash check between your TB-01 model (sections 1 and 2) and `datasets/mep_model.ifc`.
- Duct D-1 (300 x 150) crosses the three beams of bay A-B (B-1/AB, B-2/AB, B-3/AB); duct D-2 (300 x 150) crosses the west and east core walls.
- Resolve each clash with an opening in the beam or wall, at least 25 mm clear of the duct on every side. Do not move the ducts: the MEP model is not yours.
- Keep openings local: each beam keeps at least 90% of its volume.
- If your program has no clash tool, the lesson gives a short script that finds the clashes.
- Save as structure.ifc.

The numbers of section 7 that are not in the [project brief](../../brief/README.md) are given above.

## In FreeCAD

FreeCAD 1.1 has no clash detection tool; a short console check with FreeCAD's solid intersection finds the clashes, and Remove Component cuts the openings, which FreeCAD exports as IfcOpeningElement.

1. Open `tb01.FCStd` (sections 1 and 2 are what matters here).
2. 3D/BIM > Generic 3D Tools > External Reference, **File** `datasets/mep_model.ifc`. Rename it `MEP model` and keep it outside TB-01 Training Building.
3. View > Panels > Python Console. Run:
   ```python
   doc = FreeCAD.ActiveDocument
   ref = doc.getObjectsByLabel("MEP model")[0]
   els = [o for o in doc.Objects if getattr(o, "IfcType", "") in ("Beam", "Column", "Wall", "Slab")]
   for i, duct in enumerate(ref.Shape.Solids):
       for e in els:
           v = duct.common(e.Shape).Volume
           if v > 1:
               print("duct", i, "clashes with", e.Label, round(v / 1e6, 1), "litres")
   ```
   It lists five clashes: duct 0 (D-1, x 2.85 to 3.15 m, z +3.100 to +3.250) with B-1/AB, B-2/AB, B-3/AB, and duct 1 (D-2, y 10.35 to 10.65 m, z +2.600 to +2.750) with W-Core-W and W-Core-E.
4. Opening for B-1/AB: 3D/BIM > Generic 3D Tools > Box, then in the Data tab **Length** `360 mm`, **Width** `400 mm`, **Height** `210 mm`, **Placement > Position** 2820, -200, 3070. That is the duct plus 30 mm all round (25 mm clearance and a 5 mm margin), through the full beam width.
5. Select the box, then Ctrl+click B-1/AB, Modify > Remove Component.
6. Same for B-2/AB (Position 2820, 6800, 3070) and B-3/AB (Position 2820, 13800, 3070).
7. Openings in the walls for D-2: boxes **Length** `350 mm`, **Width** `360 mm`, **Height** `210 mm` at 7450, 10320, 2570 (W-Core-W) and 10200, 10320, 2570 (W-Core-E); Remove Component on each wall.
8. Run the script of step 3 again: it prints nothing. Each beam keeps more than 95 % of its volume.
9. Select TB-01 Training Building, File > Export…, `structure.ifc`. Do not move the ducts and do not export the MEP model.

## In Bonsai

1. Save your TB-01 model as `structure.ifc` (**File > Save IFC Project As...**).
2. Go to **Quality and Coordination > Clash Detection > Clash Sets** and click **Add Clash Set**. Name it `Structure vs MEP`, **Mode** intersection.
3. Next to **Group A**, click **+** (**Add Clash Source**) and pick `structure.ifc` with the folder icon. Next to **Group B**, add `datasets/mep_model.ifc`.
4. Click **Execute IFC Clash** and choose where to save the results file. The panel lists the clashes: duct D-1 Supply with beams B-1/AB, B-2/AB and B-3/AB, and duct D-2 Extract with walls W-Core-W and W-Core-E.
5. Click a clash and **Select Clash** to see where it is. Do not move the ducts.
6. Make one cutting box per clash: from the **Add** menu in the 3D viewport header choose **Mesh > Cube**, then in the sidebar (N) > **Item** set **Location** and **Dimensions**. Each box is 30 mm larger than the duct on every side:

   | Host | Location X, Y, Z | Dimensions X, Y, Z |
   |---|---|---|
   | B-1/AB | 3.0, 0.0, 3.175 | 0.36, 0.40, 0.21 |
   | B-2/AB | 3.0, 7.0, 3.175 | 0.36, 0.40, 0.21 |
   | B-3/AB | 3.0, 14.0, 3.175 | 0.36, 0.40, 0.21 |
   | W-Core-W | 7.625, 10.5, 2.675 | 0.35, 0.36, 0.21 |
   | W-Core-E | 10.375, 10.5, 2.675 | 0.35, 0.36, 0.21 |

7. Select a box, Shift-click its host beam or wall, and click **Apply Void** in the tool settings (or Shift+O with a BIM tool active). Repeat for the five boxes.
8. Save with **File > Save IFC Project**.
9. Run the clash set again with **Mode** clearance and **Clearance** `0.025`. No clashes remain.

## Check your work

Save or export your work as `structure.ifc`, then run this from the course folder:

```
python -m bimcheck 7.5 structure.ifc datasets/mep_model.ifc
```

Every line should say PASS. A FAIL line tells you what was expected and what was found.

## Why this matters

Clash detection is what Navisworks is used for, and it is the daily work of BIM coordinators. Any opening in a beam must be agreed with the structural engineer; this exercise sizes them, a real project also checks them.
