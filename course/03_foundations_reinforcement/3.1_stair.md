# Exercise 3.1: Stair from Level 0 to Level 1

**Goal.** Model a straight concrete stair through the stair opening.

## What you build

- Width 1.2 m between x 13.0 and 14.2; first riser at y 1.0; climbs in +Y.
- 20 risers of 180 mm (total rise 3.600), treads of 280 mm, arriving at Level 1 inside the opening (y up to 6.4).
- Place it in Level 0. If your program can store the number of risers and the riser height, do it.

All numbers come from the [project brief](../../brief/README.md) and its [drawings](../../brief/drawings/).

## In FreeCAD

1. Open `tb01.FCStd`. Right-click **Level 0** and tick **Active**. Click an empty part of the 3D view so nothing is selected.
2. 3D/BIM > Stairs. A straight flight appears at the origin.
3. In the Data tab set **Length** `5320 mm` (19 treads of 280), **Width** `1200 mm`, **Height** `3600 mm` and **Number Of Steps** `20`. Check that **Riser Height** shows `180 mm` and **Tread Depth** `280 mm`.
4. Leave **Align** at `Left` and **Structure** at `Massive`. Set **Placement**: **Angle** `90 °`, **Axis** 0, 0, 1, **Position** 13000, 1000, 0. The flight now runs in +Y from y 1.0 m, between x 13.0 and 14.2 m.
5. Rename it `ST-1` and check that it is inside Level 0.
6. Look at the top step: FreeCAD models 19 treads, up to +3.420. The 20th riser is the edge of the slab opening at Level 1, and the flight ends at y 6.32, inside the opening.
7. Store the riser count: select ST-1, Manage > Manage IFC Properties. In **Add property set** pick **Stair Common**. Set **NumberOfRiser** `20` and **NumberOfTreads** `19` and leave the other values empty. Press OK.
8. Do not fill **RiserHeight** there: FreeCAD 1.1.3 writes length properties in millimetres into a file whose unit is the metre, so 180 would be read as 180 m. The checker takes the riser height from the geometry.
9. Clear **Active** on Level 0. Save and export `tb01.ifc`.

## In Bonsai

Bonsai's parametric stair counts treads, not risers: 19 treads give 20 risers of 3.6 / 20 = 0.180 m. It also draws a full tread at the top, so you shorten the last one to land at y 6.4.

1. Make **Level 0** the default container.
2. From the **Add** menu in the 3D viewport header, choose **IFC Element**. In the dialog set **Name** `ST-1`, **Definition** IfcElement, **Class** IfcStairFlight, **Predefined Type** STRAIGHT, **Representation** Stair, and click OK.
3. Go to **Geometry and Materials > Parametric Geometry > Stair** and click the pencil (**Enable Editing Stair**).
4. Set **Stair Type** Concrete, **Width** `1.2`, **Height** `3.6`, **Number of Treads** `19`, **Tread Run** `0.28`, **Tread Depth** `0.25`, **Base Slab Depth** `0.25`, **Top Slab Depth** `0.25`.
5. Untick **Has Top Nib**. With it on, the waist runs on under the landing to y 6.67, past the slab edge.
6. Click **Unlock First/Last Treads** and set **Custom First / Last Treads Widths** to `0.28` and `0.08`. The last riser is then at y 6.32 and the top tread ends at y 6.40.
7. Click **Finish Editing**. The panel shows **Number of Risers** 20, **Tread Rise** 0.18 and **Length** 5.40.
8. Bonsai builds the flight along its local X, with the width along local Y. In the sidebar (N) > **Item** set **Location** `14.2`, `1.0`, `0` and **Rotation Z** `90`. The flight now climbs in +Y between x 13.0 and 14.2.
9. Look at the stair in **Object Information > Object > Property Sets**: Bonsai filled **Pset_StairFlightCommon** with NumberOfRiser 20 and RiserHeight 0.18.
10. Check from the side that the top tread meets the slab edge at +3.600. Save with **File > Save IFC Project**.

## Check your work

Save or export your work as `tb01.ifc`, then run:

```
python -m bimcheck 3.1 tb01.ifc
```

Every line should say PASS. A FAIL line tells you what was expected and what was found.

## Why this matters

Stairs are where architecture and structure meet: the structural engineer owns the flight and its supports, the architect owns the going and the headroom. A stair modelled with the right riser count is also checked against building rules (riser height, going) by code-checking tools.
