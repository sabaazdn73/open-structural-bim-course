Bonsai works in IFC directly: the file you save is `tb01.ifc`. The panels below are in the Properties editor on the right of the screen. Pick a Bonsai tab with the icons at the top of that editor (Project Overview, Object Information, Geometry and Materials, Drawings and Documents, Costing and Scheduling, Quality and Coordination). The sidebar of the 3D viewport opens with N.

1. Start Blender with Bonsai enabled. Choose **File > New IFC Project > New Metric (m) Project**. Bonsai creates an IFC4 project in metres with My Project, My Site, My Building and My Storey.
2. In the Outliner, click **IfcProject/My Project**. In **Object Information > Object > Object Attributes**, click **Edit**, set **Name** to `TB-01 Training Building` and click **Save Attributes**.
3. Do the same for **IfcSite/My Site** (Name `TB-01 Site`) and **IfcBuilding/My Building** (Name `TB-01`).
4. Select the site again and click **Edit** in **Object Attributes**. Type the location as degrees, minutes, seconds and millionths of a second, in square brackets:
   - **RefLatitude** `[38, 44, 12, 840000]` (38.7369)
   - **RefLongitude** `[-9, -8, -33, -720000]` (-9.1427, every part negative)
   - **RefElevation** `90`
5. Click **Save Attributes**.
6. Open **Project Overview > Geometry > Georeferencing** and expand its **True North** subpanel. It says "No True North Found". Click the pencil (**Enable Editing True North**).
7. Type `-12` in **Angle**. Bonsai counts angles anticlockwise, so -12 means 12 degrees clockwise. **Abscissa** becomes 0.2079 and **Ordinate** 0.9781. Click **Edit True North**.
8. Check **Project Overview > Geometry > Units**: the length unit is the metre.
9. Choose **File > Save IFC Project** (Ctrl+S) and save as `tb01.ifc` in your course folder. **File > Save** saves only the Blender file, not the IFC.

If this does not work: the site fields only accept whole numbers in the list. Check that you typed four integers, separated by commas, inside square brackets.
