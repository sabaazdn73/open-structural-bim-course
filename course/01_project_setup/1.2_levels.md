# Exercise 1.2: Levels (building storeys)

**Goal.** Create the five levels of TB-01 at their structural elevations.

## What you build

- Foundation **-1.200**, Level 0 **+0.000**, Level 1 **+3.600**, Level 2 **+7.200**, Roof **+10.800**.
- Use exactly these names; they are how the checker (and your colleagues) find the levels.
- All levels belong to the building TB-01.

All numbers come from the [project brief](../../brief/README.md) and its [drawings](../../brief/drawings/).

## In FreeCAD

1. Open `tb01.FCStd`. In the tree, expand TB-01 Training Building > TB-01 Site > TB-01. Setup Project made Level 0 to Level 3 at 0, 3600, 7200 and 10800 mm.
2. Select **Level 3**, press F2 and rename it `Roof`.
3. Click an empty part of the 3D view so that nothing is selected (the Level tool puts the selected objects inside the new level).
4. 3D/BIM > Level. A new level appears in the tree.
5. Rename it `Foundation`. In the property editor (Data tab) open **Placement > Position** and set **z** to `-1200 mm`.
6. If Foundation is not inside TB-01, drag it onto TB-01 in the tree.
7. Check each level's **Placement > Position > z**: Level 0 `0`, Level 1 `3600 mm`, Level 2 `7200 mm`, Roof `10800 mm`. FreeCAD writes this height as the storey elevation (the Elevation attribute follows it).
8. Save, select TB-01 Training Building and export `tb01.ifc` (File > Export…, Industry Foundation Classes).

## In Bonsai

1. Open `tb01.ifc` with **File > Open IFC Project** if it is not open.
2. Go to **Project Overview > Spatial > Spatial Decomposition**. The list shows the project, site, building and My Storey. The right column of each storey is its elevation.
3. Double-click **My Storey** in the list and rename it `Level 0`. Leave its elevation at 0.
4. Click the row **TB-01** (the building). In the dropdown above the list choose **IfcBuildingStorey** and click the **+** button (**Add Part to Object**). A new storey appears under the building.
5. Double-click the new storey's name and type `Foundation`. Double-click its elevation and type `-1.2`. Bonsai moves the storey to that height.
6. Repeat steps 4 and 5 for `Level 1` at `3.6`, `Level 2` at `7.2` and `Roof` at `10.8`.
7. Check that the five storeys are listed under TB-01 and nothing else is.
8. Click the **Level 0** row and click **Set Default**. New elements go into the default storey, and the panel header shows "Default: Level 0".
9. Save with **File > Save IFC Project**.

If this does not work: if a storey ends up under the site or the project, delete it with the **X** button (**Delete Container**), click the building row again and add it once more.

## Check your work

Save or export your work as `tb01.ifc`, then run:

```
python -m bimcheck 1.2 tb01.ifc
```

Every line should say PASS. A FAIL line tells you what was expected and what was found.

## Why this matters

In IFC a level is an *IfcBuildingStorey*: every element is placed in one, and schedules, drawings and quantities are organised by it. Structural levels are usually at the top of the structural slab, which is a few centimetres below the architect's finished floor level; agree which one you model before you start.
